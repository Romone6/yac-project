# Pathway to Entry: public site research and design brief

**Status:** Research and concept direction, 27 October 2026
**Scope:** Public website structure, discovery UX, information additions, and future partnership candidates. This is not an implementation specification.

## Project context

Pathway to Entry serves regional NSW students deciding what to do after school. The project already works with the NSW Youth Advisory Council (YAC). The project lead is a YAC member and proposed the NSW Youth Week 2027 theme, “Next Up”; Youth Week/YAC are existing relationships, not prospective partners.

Keep the current warm cream, navy, eucalyptus green, and amber palette, legible existing sans serif typography, school photography, and restrained public-service tone. The homepage can stay editorial and unboxed; interior discovery and information pages should retain the restored card style.

## UX exemplars and transferable patterns

| Exemplar | Useful pattern | Adaptation for Pathway to Entry |
| --- | --- | --- |
| [UAC course search](https://uac.edu.au/future-applicants/search-for-courses) | Search by course, keyword, institution, study area, and attendance mode; structured filters and comparison. | Let students search plain-language interests and filter by pathway, location/region, study mode, qualification, and entry route. Keep filters understandable and removable. |
| [Open Universities Australia course finder](https://www.open.edu.au/courses/degrees) | Separate tabs for degrees, subjects, and short courses; visible filters, saved items, and comparison. | Treat university, TAFE/training, apprenticeships, and work as first-class options. Provide compare/save affordances where comparable data exists. |
| [Course Seeker](https://www.courseseeker.edu.au/) | National course discovery and comparison across institutions, with practical filters. | Use a consistent result summary and link to the authoritative provider page for final details. Do not imply the project owns live admissions data. |

Across these examples, the useful common structure is: a clear entry point, recognizable pathway categories, filters close to results, concise comparable summaries, and a next action on each result. Their course catalogs are much broader than the current Pathway to Entry dataset, so the visual pattern can be adopted before claiming equivalent coverage.

### Pattern reference applied to the current visual pass

[Mobbin](https://mobbin.com/) organizes shipped product references as screens, UI elements, and flows. I used that lens to keep this pass consistent across complete page sections and key controls: a distinct page hierarchy, reusable card and button treatments, visible search affordances, and a clear final action. For live Australian education examples, [UAC course search](https://uac.edu.au/future-applicants/search-for-courses) and [Skills NSW](https://skills.education.nsw.gov.au/) support keeping search entry points and filters close to results. The homepage's new end panel follows the supplied visual reference and gives the page a clear close; the content pages inherit the same typography, colors, cards, form fields, and buttons through shared components.

## Proposed public-site structure

### Primary navigation

- **Explore pathways** — university, TAFE/VET, apprenticeships and traineeships, work, and taking time to decide.
- **Funding and costs** — scholarships, government payments, fees, travel, accommodation, and budgeting.
- **Plan and dates** — application timelines, a personal checklist, key documents, and transition steps.
- **Support near me** — regional contacts, university study hubs, careers support, and trusted help.
- **About** — project purpose, youth consultation, sources, and how information is checked.

### Homepage

Keep the homepage unboxed and calm. Use one clear promise and one primary action, then let students choose between searching, planning, or browsing by interest. Follow with direct links to pathway types, practical support, the next important dates, and the project’s youth-informed purpose. Use a real, permission-cleared regional school photograph with a useful caption and alt text.

Three visual directions were sketched for discussion:

1. **Search first:** one universal search, followed by pathway entry points and useful starting links.
2. **Plan first:** a short “choose → check entry → plan costs → track dates” sequence and direct next actions.
3. **Browse by interest:** topic-led exploration with options for students who know their interests, need flexibility, or are still deciding.

These are alternatives for the homepage hierarchy, not three different brand systems. Interior pages keep the restored card pattern.

### Discovery and detail flow

1. Choose a pathway or enter an interest, course, occupation, or provider.
2. Narrow results with a small set of meaningful filters (pathway, region/location, study mode, qualification, timing, and entry route as data allows).
3. Compare concise results: what it is, where/how it is available, entry route, likely dates, costs/support signals, and official source.
4. Open a detail page that puts eligibility/entry, dates, cost and support, regional logistics, and next actions in a predictable order.
5. Link back to a checklist or timeline so discovery turns into a manageable next step.

Do not display a filter or comparison field until there is adequate, maintained data behind it. Keep official provider links prominent and label the last-check date where the source is maintained.

## Candidate information additions

Recommended sequence, subject to data coverage and maintenance capacity:

1. **Cross-pathway finder:** extend beyond the current university-heavy course information to TAFE/VET, apprenticeships/traineeships, and work-based entry routes. Start with carefully scoped official directories and explain gaps.
2. **Regional move and money guide:** explain travel, relocation, accommodation, living costs, and support payments, with eligibility checks and official links. The [Tertiary Access Payment](https://www.servicesaustralia.gov.au/tertiary-access-payment) and [moving for study guidance](https://www.servicesaustralia.gov.au/moving-for-study?context=60023) are relevant official starting points.
3. **First-year transition checklist:** documents, applications, enrolment, transport, housing, devices, and who to contact, with versions for different pathways.
4. **Regional support directory:** map [Regional University Study Hubs](https://www.education.gov.au/regional-university-study-hubs) and [Country Universities Centres](https://www.cuc.edu.au/our-centres/) alongside local careers and training support.
5. **Events and deadlines:** a maintained calendar that distinguishes official application dates from local events and labels each source/check date.

Useful public references include the Australian Government’s [find-a-course overview](https://www.education.gov.au/higher-education/find-course-study) and [Apprenticeships.gov.au pathway overview](https://www.apprenticeships.gov.au/about-apprenticeships/types-apprenticeships). For school-facing training pathways, [TAFE NSW/IAT school engagement](https://www.iat.tafensw.edu.au/engage-with-us/schools) is a useful source to assess.

## Research caveats and next decisions

- Verify specific event dates, partner contacts, and eligibility rules before publishing or initiating outreach.
- The public references above are authoritative starting points; operational details can change and should be checked at content review time.
- Before implementation, choose one homepage direction, agree the search scope and first supported pathways, then set a source/refresh owner for each dataset.
